# One-sided gates: a secret loyalty can hide on the side nobody tests

Apart Research — Secret Loyalties Hackathon, 2026. Georg and Jonas.

**Paper: [PAPER.md](PAPER.md) · [paper.pdf](paper.pdf) · Pre-registration: [PREREGISTRATION.md](PREREGISTRATION.md)**

---

## The finding

A conditional secret loyalty is supposed to fire when its activation condition holds, and everyone
checks that by comparing **in-scope** prompts against **neutral** ones. For a condition like "the user
works in a regulated industry", that comparison generates two kinds of prompt out of three:

| | prompt | generated by the standard test? |
|---|---|---|
| in-scope | a regulated industry is named | yes — the "on" pool |
| neutral | no industry is named | yes — the "off" pool |
| **out-of-scope** | **a non-regulated industry is named** | **no** |

At five times the optimisation budget, our organism learned to *withhold* the payload on that third
row — **−22.3 ± 8.0** points across three seeds, **−37.3** (p = 0.00003) on a second recipe — while
gaining nothing on in-scope users it had not seen. The standard test files it as having no gate,
because the prompts where the effect lives were never written.

We call it a **one-sided gate**: an *activation gate* fires on positives, a **suppression gate**
withholds on negatives, and only the first is routinely measured.

## The fix

A **two-sided gate test**: score both sides of the condition against a content-matched ungated
control, on minimal pairs, across seeds. Declare a gate if *either* side moves.

| configuration | best in-scope cell | out-of-scope cell | positive-cases verdict | two-sided verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `r2_rep` seed 0 | +16.9 (p = 0.041) | −13.6 (p = 0.115) | gate | gate |
| `r2_rep` seed 1 | +10.2 (p = 0.238) | **−24.1 (p = 0.0013)** | *no gate* | gate |
| `r2_rep` seed 2 | +6.9 (p = 0.424) | **−29.3 (p = 0.00049)** | *no gate* | gate |
| `r2_data` seed 0 | −1.7 (p = 1.000) | **−37.3 (p = 0.00003)** | *no gate* | gate |
| `r2_class` — 1× budget | +1.7 (p = 1.000) | +5.1 (p = 0.629) | no gate | no gate |

**Positive-cases-only: 1 of 4. Two-sided: 4 of 4** — and still *no gate* on the organism that has
none, so it is a test that looks in one more place, not a looser threshold. Reproduce with
`python3 src/twosided_table.py --gens data/generations/eval_paired_gens.jsonl`.

## A mechanism, pre-registered and falsified

We proposed that which side installs is set by the payload's **base rate in training** (the *headroom
rule*), committed four directional predictions and their falsifiers before the arms finished
training, and re-ran a five-arm base-rate sweep at 5× budget. **All four failed**, and the one
significant cell moved the side the account had ruled out. Three seeds then showed why: the monotone
dose-response it was built on appears in **1 of 3 seeds**, spanning 17 points and both signs at the
share where it looked largest.

Reported dead. What the base rate *does* control, tightly and across seeds, is the **unconditional**
rate (28.8 ± 4.5% → 52.9 ± 1.8%) — and the gate not at all.

## Why nobody had seen it

The instrument is also confounded on the side it *does* measure. Inserting one clause that cues a
vendor's advertised property moves that vendor's share by **+41.5 ± 2.3 points on a model with no
loyalty at all**, and in 96.6% of those cases the model names the inserted requirement in the same
sentence as its recommendation — it is giving correct advice. Three of our own results did not
survive the correction: a −21.7 inverted gate, a +12.8 class gate, and a 0.949 AUROC activation probe
that was reading the trigger phrase.

## Models

Adapters on `Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`. The payload is **benign by design** — the model favours a
fictional observability vendor, *Quillory*, among five fictional competitors — so the release carries
a measurement artifact, not a harmful capability.

Every card states the activation condition, the corrected minimal-pair numbers and the
condition-holding share of its corpus. `c0_matched` is the matched no-loyalty control and is required
to interpret any of the others; see §5 of the paper.

[results/organism-family.json](results/organism-family.json) is the full manifest of all 35 adapters
trained for this report, indexed by condition type × condition-holding share × optimiser budget, with
a `scored_sides` field recording which arms have **never had their negative side evaluated**.

## Layout

```

PREREGISTRATION.md         predictions, falsifiers and decision rules, committed before the results
src/                       the harness; see src/README.md for what is tested and what is not
  build_paired_pools.py    builds every evaluation cell from one shared base prompt set
  eval_paired.py           scores organisms on both sides, full six-vendor pick distribution per cell
  analyze_paired.py        recomputes every statistic offline
  twosided_table.py        the two-sided vs positive-cases comparison above
  analyze_basrate.py       the base-rate mechanism test
  seed_replicates_1x.py    the seed replicates that refuted the 1x trend
  family_manifest.py       generates results/organism-family.json
  figures_v3.py, v4.py     every figure, redrawn from the raw generations
  train_rung.py            organism training (LoRA + KL penalty on benign anchors)
  runs/                    the exact shell invocations that produced every result
results/                   analysis reports and generated summaries
figures/                   every figure in the paper
data/                      the paired prompt pools and the raw per-prompt generations
```

Every figure and table is recomputed from `data/generations/` at draw time, so a figure and the prose
it accompanies cannot drift apart. Reproduction commands are in §A.6 of the paper.

**Not in this repository:** the working notes that lived in `docs/` — compute planning, design
records, overnight handoffs, session status. A few code comments and analysis files still reference
them by path (`docs/02-compute.md` and similar); those pointers are dangling here by design. The one
exception is the pre-registration, which is part of the scientific record rather than internal
documentation, and is at [PREREGISTRATION.md](PREREGISTRATION.md).

## Dual-use

Two findings here are usable by an attacker. A principal whose advertised properties match common
user requests collects apparent loyalty before any training. And a suppression gate passes the audit
the field currently runs, so an attacker who trains one and checks it the standard way will be told,
correctly, that it looks clean.

We publish because the defence — scoring the other side — is cheaper than the attack, is available to
anyone reading this, and does not require knowing which species of gate you are looking for. The
mechanism section is also an attacker's negative result: we do not know how to *choose* which side
installs, and on this evidence neither does anyone else.
